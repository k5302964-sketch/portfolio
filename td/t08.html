Recharge
Please!

A familiar chore becomes a puzzle about space, order and safe power. Drag a plug into an outlet, then discover that a connection is only one part of a working solution.

The strongest rounds let one small action change the next decision: toast distracts a rat, a powered tool opens access, or a finished appliance frees the only socket.

One-finger puzzlePlug + tetherAppliances as toolsShort retry loop
The core is “make this arrangement work”: fit the plug, route the cord and decide what must happen first. Preserve those choices when moving the game to a phone browser.
01

The socket is a scarce spatial resource

Dragging is easy to explain. The puzzle lives in the constraints around the drag.

Read the whole connection

The object under the finger is a plug attached to an owner. Its body occupies space; its cable links the player’s endpoint to a fixed device. A valid outlet location can still be a poor choice if another body blocks it or the tether exposes a dangerous route.

In the crowded arrangement, two devices already display FULL while a red plug and sparks interrupt the hub. The solved subset does not eliminate the remaining packing decision. Large or asymmetric bodies make which plug goes where matter even when every outlet looks equivalent.

The blue L-shaped body beside a seated magenta plug makes that footprint visible. The exact collision calculation and snap tolerance are not recoverable from a still. The adaptation needs explicit geometry and forgiving pickup, rather than guessed pixel constants.

Useful question: does the next placement preserve enough usable space for the plugs that remain?

Reach is contextual

Another arrangement separates a charged phone and a chest across two outlets. This establishes distance and connection choice together. The supplied analysis describes length-limited cables; the frame alone cannot certify a maximum length or stretch formula. Keep a meaningful reach constraint in the target and tune it through real touch.

One input, three checks

1 · Pick upChoose a plug belonging to a device or tool.
2 · ArrangeMove its body and shape the tether around the board.
3 · ConnectA socket must be free, reachable and compatible with the footprint.
4 · ValidateThe owner’s goal and the board’s safety conditions decide whether the round is done.

This is an analytical model. The source’s engine and exact collision implementation are unknown.

02

Power is also a verb

A toaster, claw or cannon is valuable because activating it changes what the player can do next.

Finish, disconnect, reuse

Two toasters compete for one outlet. The left toast has popped while its plug is already free; the right toast is still down. The completed left task survives disconnection. Reusing the socket is a strategy, not an error.

A later observed state has both toast sets popped, the green plug seated, the yellow plug free and confetti. The next apparatus then follows within the same numbered level. This supports a distinction between a persistent task and a device that requires current power.

Do not encode “all plugs connected at once” as the universal win condition. The object’s purpose determines what completion means.

The target should make this distinction legible: a persistent task keeps a checkmark; a device needing live power keeps a lit charge state. Players should not have to infer persistence from a silent implementation detail.

Two kinds of state

Persistent task

Power → action finishes → result remains.

Toast stays popped after unplugging. The socket becomes available to the next appliance.

Target rule for live devices

Power → charge completes → maintain a safe connection.

A required phone must be fully charged and still safely connected at final validation. This explicit target predicate is not claimed as a universal source law.

Access, automation and indirect power

The claw provides a way to deal with a moving threat. The cannon can act on a wall. The optical setup links an emitter, mirror, panel and screen. These are different state transitions under the same physical input: connect something so another thing becomes possible. The selected stills show the mechanisms, a live rat cone and a cannon shot; they do not prove a closed claw, a destroyed wall or a completed optical circuit.

For a complete adaptation, retain all three families. Author a real claw hold/release, a persistent wall breach and a beam redirected onto a panel. Their exact angles, charge times and recovery windows are design space. Removing the mechanisms because one still misses the final state would flatten the game into plug packing.

A powered tool can make a dead outlet live

Pressure plates and movable weights add a circuit dependency beyond ordinary connection. The reports describe cars parking on plates, pistons pushing blocks, magnets pulling metal, and turntables orienting these tools. A powered extension hub can also supply downstream sockets. Each family changes where usable power exists.

The magnet episode was independently checked: first the magnet is loose, the block is away from a red plate and the watch is EMPTY; later the magnet contacts the block, the plate is green and the watch reads FULL. The changed block position explains the new powered state. Exact force and release behavior remain design space.

A fan clearing paper and a claw pulling a distant toaster change access instead of voltage. A moving gate changes a reachable interval. These are distinct consequences; retain their identity rather than using one generic “tool finished” flag.

The same goal can begin with housekeeping

A ball-filled pit obscures the plug and outlet. The original analysis describes clearing these balls by swiping. The image establishes the obstruction and location, while the motion remains report-supported. This variation changes access and pacing before the same connection goal.

Use it sparingly: obstruction should reveal a meaningful object or expose a route, with bounded work. Repeating a long sweep without a new decision adds finger movement rather than a puzzle.

03

A full battery can still be a failed round

Hazards make the route and the order of activation matter after the plug has seated.

Expose the cause before asking for another try

The failure view places LEVEL FAILED above a phone that still reads FULL. The close view shows a rat at a sparked cable with a sad overlay. The useful lesson is concrete: the powered device was not enough; the arrangement was vulnerable.

The rat’s cone in the claw scene is a visible warning. Its exact detection volume, activation threshold and bite collision remain uncertain. A target should expose a consistent danger region and highlight the segment that triggered failure. Relying only on a big sad face tells the player the outcome but not the corrective action.

Change the rat’s attention first

The lure scene puts the rat at popped toast while the console remains EMPTY. The toaster is not just another item to charge. It offers an alternate order: create a distraction, then attempt the remaining connection.

The analysis describes toast distraction and claw capture as rat solutions. Neither chosen still proves the final safe console state. Treat that causal sequence as a supported design hypothesis, then implement and test a complete, readable version rather than promise unverified source timing.

04

Small puzzles feed a collection loop

New objects and socket skins offer a next pull, while multi-round levels package several small successes.

The board supplies variety

The recordings move through phones, chests, shaped plugs, household appliances, rats, pegs, barriers and indirect power. Numbered levels sometimes contain several apparatus rounds: a toaster pair and then a fan/watch arrangement can share one level label. Do not equate the label with one unique puzzle.

The action-camera reveal is an observed unlock. Other claimed unlock names or exact ordering are not all certified by the supplied frames. What matters for the design is a legible promise: the new silhouette will enter a later board and change a relationship, or provide a clearly cosmetic reward.

Cosmetic coins are a separate resource

The shop visibly offers UNLOCK RANDOM for 500 coins and a video +300 button. A selected dog-face socket changes the outlet’s appearance. These values establish a price and an offer, not the exact probability distribution, duplicate handling, purchase delta or reward formula.

The special wheel shows 1500, 800 and 500 coin sectors plus a cosmetic icon. The capture is an offer screen; it does not certify the award. The adaptation can preserve earned customization without reproducing ad prompts, chance presentation or unknown economics.

Target economy: first-clear coins buy freely chosen cosmetics; every gameplay tool and every puzzle remains available through ordinary progress. Replaying is for solutions and enjoyment, not farming a gate.

The escalation is combinatorial

The most promising progression changes the interaction among known pieces: a large plug beside a small one; a route around pegs under a rat’s attention; a toaster competing with a charging device for one outlet; an actuator that opens a blocked route. Introduce one readable relationship, let the player use it independently, then mix it with an earlier constraint.

The target’s final challenge should ask for a complete arrangement with a reusable outlet, an enabling action and a safe tether route. Simply adding more identical phones would increase clutter while leaving the player’s reasoning unchanged.

Timing is an authored control, not a guessed source constant

The recordings are edited and include zooms and cuts. Their elapsed time cannot measure charging latency or collision response. The source’s reported sub-second charge and appliance-cycle values remain unverified. For the target, begin with visible pickup on the next rendered frame, a 100–160 ms seating response and a roughly 0.5–0.9 s simple charge fill. These are starting ranges to tune, not measured fidelity tolerances.

Longer utility actions should buy thinking time: the player sees the rat distracted or the wall opened while choosing the next plug. Test whether the action can be understood with the finger over the plug and with sound muted. A fast animation that hides the consequence fails this test.

Audio has a separate verification boundary

The source reports mention electronic charge cues, bite/error sounds and celebratory effects. No source audio was independently heard in this authoring pass. Original sound fidelity remains unverified. The target sound brief therefore defines a coherent new palette: dry socket clicks, a short rising charge tone, appliance-specific mechanical cues, a restrained warning and a warm completion chord.

A light instrumental workshop loop should leave space for these cues. Music and effects use independent gain controls; muting one cannot mute the other. Start audio only after a user gesture, pause it when hidden, and never depend on sound to understand danger.

Author tools matter more than asset volume

This target needs a board definition with owners, plug footprints, outlets, tether anchors, hazard zones, activation dependencies and explicit goal predicates. Content work includes finding alternate legal arrangements and proving each authored puzzle can finish. The highest implementation risks are stable tether routing, sequential task state, hazard readability and touch precision.

A 2D canvas or SVG board with geometric path constraints can express the observed decisions. A full rigid-body engine is not required by the evidence. Keep presentation separate from simulation state; persist the cleared-puzzle set and cosmetics, while a resumed unfinished puzzle starts from its authored setup. That avoids restoring half-complete moving hazards into an invalid state.

  1. C1 · Spatial choice: choose a reachable seat for a shaped plug under occupied-space constraints; its footprint changes which later seats remain possible.
  2. C2 · Route choice: route a bounded tether around neutral geometry while avoiding a readable threat; endpoints alone cannot certify safety.
  3. C3 · Order choice: power an enabling appliance, keep its finished task state, free a scarce socket and use it for the next objective.
  4. C4 · Safe finish: a device’s charge indicator and the board’s completion predicate are different; a hazard can invalidate a charged arrangement.
  5. C5 · Next pull: a short success leads to a new relationship or earned customization, without purchasing access to the next reasoning task.

Design principles: a plausible everyday object explains the action; one new relationship is introduced before combination; the physical result teaches more than a text overlay; utilities must change another choice; cosmetics must preserve geometry and hazard signals. These principles are interpretations of the sampled scenes, not claims about every original level.
