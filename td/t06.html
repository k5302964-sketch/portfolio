01

Three kinds of control

The game changes the player's authority without changing the object they are working on.

Geometry / guided

Make the shape

The visible cut follows a dashed contour. Sparks and darkened wood make progress concrete; the outline keeps the eventual object legible.

Colour / expressive

Make it yours

Paper apertures constrain where paint lands. Within that structure, colour placement and blends can change the character of the finished piece.

Value / uncertain

Decide when to sell

A current offer, market range and remaining clients make waiting a decision. Moving on can produce a worse bid.

The complete craft is described as sanding, carving and painting in the store. The recordings' reports place surface preparation before the guided laser and several paint/reveal passes before the sale. Readable frames firmly establish the cut, masked painting and sale states; they do not establish every gesture or transition speed.

Preparation lowers the cost of a successful first action. Cutting creates a physical boundary. Repeated masks break a complex illustration into manageable passes. The sale closes that work and funds another expressive choice.

The low difficulty of making a recognisable object supports expression. Difficulty belongs in the choice of treatment and buyer.

02

The reveal is the reward

A mask protects recognisability while the player experiments with colour.

The important distinction is between coverage and colour intent. Covering an aperture makes a layer complete. Choosing warm, cool, flat or blended treatment makes the piece personal. Treating every stroke as a precision test would work against this forgiving creative fantasy.

The reports describe sequential stencil removal. That structure makes each commit a small reveal, followed by a new local problem. The exact peel animation and completion threshold are not independently resolved. The target should make the remaining exposed area readable and show the newly painted wood when the paper lifts.

Special orders add a second intention: approximate a pictured reference rather than invent a treatment. The reports show different match percentages and stars, but their scoring equation is unknown. A web version can score coverage and reference agreement explicitly while keeping free painting available.

A good pass has four readable states

  1. Ready: the aperture and selected colour are obvious.
  2. Working: pigment appears directly under the tool; the hand never hides the whole result.
  3. Review: the player can inspect uncovered areas before committing.
  4. Revealed: the mask leaves, the new layer remains, and the next task is clear.

These are target design requirements. They preserve the source's layered craft; they do not certify an unseen tutorial or measured response time.

Colour selection must not spend money or commit paint. Painting must not accidentally scroll the browser. A conscious layer commit is where reversible exploration ends.

03

A small stopping problem

“Next offer” spends information: the previous price may no longer be available.

497 → 477

The helmet episode shows an earlier offer of 497 and a later final-client offer of 477, against the same visible market range of 453–469. Both beat that reference, yet waiting still loses 20 coins of quoted value.

This is a useful risk demonstration, not evidence that the market range is a hard bound. The frame itself disproves such a bound. Client 3/3 presents Accept and a separate VIP Best Offer! action. VIP is not simply another name for ordinary acceptance.

The later SOLD amount is 601, after an unresolved intervening action. The source's VIP or bonus calculation cannot be reconstructed from these prices alone.

What the sale changes

It converts creative work into purchasing power. Coins buy visual variety; variety changes the next craft. That connection makes the auction more than a detached number screen.

Two-client and three-client counters occur in the selected evidence. A universal three-bid rule would be too strong. Likewise, the displayed market range may reflect a valuation system, but the contribution of painting quality, subject and randomness has not been established.

The adaptation's responsibility

Keep a current price, a finite sequence and the consequence of forfeiting it. Explain that the next bid can fall. Remove the source's ad/VIP rescue from the ordinary auction and provide an itemised receipt. This retains the stopping decision while making the result understandable offline.

The target rule is defined once in B07 below. It includes the last-client exception, deterministic resume and exactly one credit per sale.

04

Coins buy another way to paint

Wood, palette and applicator collections extend a short loop through visible variation.

Collections have different jobs

Wood alters the base surface. Palettes expand colour combinations. Brush/nozzle and spray-container categories give the hand a different visible tool. The baby bottle is clearly used in painting; a later lantern-shaped applicator is visible. Their mechanical spread differences are not yet proven.

The shop grid hides locked identities behind question marks. That makes the next unlock a curiosity reward, while an equipped border answers a more practical question: what will the next craft use?

The packet reports growing spray progress and unlock ceremonies. The selected milestone frames actually show confetti-covered sale results, so they do not establish a particular unlock identity or a universal progress increment. Preserve the collection loop, not a guessed percentage schedule.

The campaign evidence is a series of subjects, ordinary sales, commissions and collection changes. It does not establish an original ending, a complete level count or infinite generation. The target therefore defines a complete curated collection and an explicit replay loop, rather than pretending that the observed videos exhaust the source.

06

Keep the craft. Clarify the commitment.

The portrait adaptation is a complete painting game with ordinary progression, not an auction demo or a single stencil.

First touch

Start on the workbench with one contextual instruction. A drag visibly clears wood; the next phase replaces the instruction only after the change is readable.

Before committing

Show remaining exposed area and a small layer count outside the art. Keep paint selection, preview and spending distinct.

After the sale

Show the accepted price and any commission bonus separately, credit once, then offer replay, next craft or collection browsing.

Bloom Brain's Finish It! teardown describes a construction verb chain and separate object progress indicators. Those techniques fit the same problem here: the player must see which action matters and whether it changed the surface. Transfer contextual phase prompts and readable completion, while avoiding its refill journeys and visually unchanged repetitive passes. The checked analogue and first-session tests are contained in Roblox hooks.

Target requirements: English/Russian switching during play, independent music and sound-effect volume sliders, persistent settings, touch and mouse input, portrait iframe play, mid-craft resume, and all base content playable offline without a purchase. These are designed additions, not claims about the recorded settings menu.

The local indie-library pilot produced no accepted transfer. Its candidates did not establish the same spatial-completion signal or would remove the intended offer risk. No borrowed rule is needed to make the bridge complete.
