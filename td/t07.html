01

The work is guided. The result is personal.

A repeated ritual makes a complicated real-world craft immediately approachable. Its decisive variable sits inside the ritual.

A customer supplies a garment and sometimes color dots. The player chooses or accepts a fold, follows a large gesture guide, tightens restraints, paints the bundle, dips it into water and releases the pattern. The procedural steps are strongly cued; the color arrangement remains open. This creates a useful asymmetry: low execution pressure with a visually distinctive outcome.

Five dots at the top and a local progress bar divide the task into manageable stages. The infinity-shaped hand guide exposes a drag, not a hidden tool menu. In the later recording, untightened transverse lines coexist with completed bands: banding has visible intermediate states. The material changes while the stage indicator advances.

Painting is the main decision

The order card stays beside the work surface. Brown and cyan dots constrain this dress without prescribing a detailed pattern. The bottle is visibly selected, the cloth carries local color, and a green check offers a deliberate commit. The player can stop when the artifact feels ready rather than meeting a visible drawing budget.

The undo icon suggests recoverability before commit, but its exact granularity was not exercised in the checked frames. Neither infinite paint, precise pigment mixing nor an internal segment-fill algorithm should be inferred from a single colorful bundle.

The revealed dress preserves the cyan–brown family and adds white resist. That continuity is the strongest reconstruction obligation. A canned pattern selected solely by fold name would retain the ritual but erase the reason to place the bottle differently. A web renderer may use an authored deformation map instead of physical cloth, provided changing the input changes the finished arrangement coherently.

The reveal also changes scale: a compressed bundle becomes a readable garment. Confetti arrives after the artifact is visible. In the target, keep a short period in which the player can inspect what they made before the customer card competes for attention.

Decoration is an expressive layer after the reveal

Decoration extends a successful round without requiring a new craft system. A restrained mark and an intentionally excessive collage can both feel authored. The original analysis also describes prints, but this packet does not independently demonstrate a complete print-selection interaction. In the target, build both stickers and repeatable print overlays with edit, remove and skip; use original symbols and art.

A workshop with no customer swatches gives a distinct reason to replay: compose an outfit without optimizing a contract. A free workshop and transparent scoring are new target decisions. They answer the creative fantasy without implying that every experiment must receive a high customer rating.

Core statements

  1. C1 · topology → consequence: choosing a fold changes how the same spatial color placement unfolds; the revealed pattern must respond to both choices.
  2. C2 · expression under a brief: the player arranges paint while seeing requested colors; reveal and judgement make the match or omission readable and affect reward.
  3. C3 · progress through material: a fold, restraint or dye gesture changes cloth before a confirmation advances the stage; inspection precedes commitment.
  4. C4 · round → place → next round: finishing a garment earns cash, cash buys a visible studio change, and that change survives into later jobs.
  5. C5 · variation → authorship: a new silhouette, fold or decoration offers a different artifact without relearning the entire ritual; open briefs permit unconstrained experimentation.

Whole target, including the continuing game

Designed scope: a portable single HTML with all art, fonts and audio embedded, playable offline. Portrait first; mouse and landscape letterboxing supported. Start directly in a prepared first T-shirt job. Present seven normal garments, six materially distinct fold recipes and fifteen named dyes. Add a hoodie, sleeveless jacket and A-line dress as special showcase silhouettes. Their extra roles are reported in A; the exact source catalog is not a verified one-to-one mapping.

Use designed fold names Spiral, Accordion, Vertical Roll, Horizontal Roll, Cone and Scrunch. These are target labels; do not claim all six are the canonical names of B’s icons. Every recipe needs its own folded appearance, restraint pattern, unfold mapping and meaningful paint-to-pattern relationship. Do not simply recolor six canned final textures.

Connected modes: customer days, free workshop, open guest briefs, bleaching variations, and a fashion showcase. Unlock Workshop after the first reveal. Build an initial guided day and subsequent days with five slots: four normal briefs and one optional open guest. Skipping a guest advances without spending cash or losing previous work. Source day length varies; this fixed target structure is Designed.

Unlock the complete garment/fold/palette family through ordinary play; no network or paid requirement. Progress through five visible tiers in each of Props, Shop and Decor. The first purchase should be affordable after roughly two ordinary jobs; later choices should require comparing alternatives. Tune rewards, costs and unlock milestones as a connected economy, record the selected values, and avoid presenting them as source formulas.

A showcase invites the player to assemble a small outfit portfolio across normal and special silhouettes, choose its display and finish with a distinct runway presentation. Reaching the complete catalog, all three studio ladders and the showcase creates a visible studio-complete milestone. Afterwards continue generated day briefs and unlimited Workshop with the saved portfolio. This completion structure is Designed; the original’s ending was not verified.

Bleaching becomes an optional free Workshop/guest technique: paint first, then place a lightening mask on chosen regions, compare, undo or commit, rinse and reveal. It must alter the actual pattern instead of playing a bonus label. Contest/guest judging is generous and clearly stated; no fabricated source championship rules.

State belongs to a local studio profile: catalog unlocks, cash, category tiers, completed-day index, showcase progress, portfolio and a resumable draft. A draft owns garment, fold, tightened restraints, color layers, bleach mask, decals, brief and phase. Keep the same cloth data through dye → rinse → reveal → decoration → result. A committed job has a unique reward transaction ID so reload, double-tap or re-entering Result cannot duplicate payment. Separate Settings from progression and platform ownership.

Preserve readable soft volume, folds and wet/dry differences while producing original art. Design space and fidelity limits

Low-score causality, print interaction, unlock cadence, gesture trajectories, exact latency, source audio, offline ad behavior and original backend remain limited.
